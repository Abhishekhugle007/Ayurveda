The history of Ayurveda

Ayurveda, "the science of life", is one of the world's oldest medical traditions. Here is how it grew.

  1. Origins in tradition

    Divine knowledge

    Classical texts say healing knowledge passed from Brahma to Daksha Prajapati, then to the Ashvini Kumaras and Indra, and on to sages such as Bharadvaja and Atreya. Dhanvantari is honoured as the physician of the gods.

  2. c. 1500 to 500 BCE

    The Vedic period

    The Rigveda and Atharvaveda hold hymns about healing plants, diet and the Ashvins' cures. These are the roots of Ayurveda.

  3. c. 600 BCE to 500 CE

    The classical age

    The Charaka Samhita set out internal medicine, diagnosis and the doshas. The Sushruta Samhita described surgery, instruments and wound care. Scholars still debate exact dates, as both grew over centuries.

  4. c. 600 CE

    Vagbhata's synthesis

    Vagbhata joined the earlier schools in the Ashtanga Hridayam, which is still studied in Ayurvedic colleges.

  5. c. 700 to 1600 CE

    Medieval refinement

    Later authors added disease classification, mineral and metal preparations and pulse reading, in works such as the Madhava Nidana and Bhavaprakasha.

  6. 1800s to 1900s

    Decline and revival

    Colonial rule favoured Western medicine, but vaidyas and schools kept the tradition alive. In 1970 India passed the Indian Medicine Central Council Act to regulate Ayurvedic education.

  7. Today

    Ayurveda worldwide

    India's Ministry of AYUSH was created in 2014, and the WHO set up its Global Centre for Traditional Medicine in Jamnagar, Gujarat. National Ayurveda Day is marked on Dhanvantari Jayanti.
